Stop 1 · The galaxy

The Milky Way

100,000 light-years across. Somewhere between 100 and 400 billion stars — and our entire solar system is one tiny glowing dot in one of its spiral arms. That pulsing marker is us.

Stop 2 · Between the stars

The stellar neighborhood

Leaving the galaxy behind, we pass our Sun's neighbours: Proxima Centauri (4.25 light-years, the closest star of all), Alpha Centauri (4.37), Sirius (8.6 — the brightest star in our night sky), and the red supergiant Betelgeuse (~640 ly away, big enough to swallow Jupiter's orbit).

Stop 3 · The ghost shell

The Oort Cloud

Before we reach the Sun, we cross a ghost shell: trillions of icy bodies orbiting 2,000–100,000 AU out — so distant that light takes over a year to cross it. This is where long-period comets fall from. No probe has ever reached it; even Voyager 1 won't arrive for another ~300 years.

Stop 4 · The star

Sol — the Sun

99.86% of all mass in the solar system. 5,500°C at the surface, 15 million°C at the core — 1.3 million Earths could fit inside it. Everything from here on orbits this furnace.

Humans are touching itParker Solar Probe · NASA 2018Aditya-L1 · ISRO 2023

Parker is the fastest object ever built — 692,000 km/h through the corona. Aditya-L1 parks 1.5 million km away, watching the Sun full-time.

Stop 5 · Planet I

Mercury

The swift planet — a year here lasts just 88 days. No atmosphere to hold heat, so it swings from −180°C at night to 430°C by day. No moons.

Stop 6 · Planet II

Venus

The hottest planet — 465°C, hotter than Mercury, wrapped in crushing CO₂ skies. It spins backwards, and one Venusian day outlasts its entire year. No moons.

Stop 7 · Planet III · Home

Earth

The only known world with life — 71% ocean, one magnetic shield, one perfect distance from the Sun.

Knocked on its side — it rolls around the Sun at a 98° tilt. Methane paints it teal, and its atmosphere plunges to −224°C, the coldest of any planet.

Stop 14 · Pluto's neighborhood

The Kuiper Belt

We never left the debris behind — Pluto lives inside it. A donut of icy worlds 30–55 AU out, with 3,000+ known objects: Eris, Haumea, Makemake… Pluto is just the most famous resident.

Stop 15 · Interstellar

The Voyagers

Voyager 1 (NASA, 1977) is the farthest human-made thing in existence — 15+ billion miles out, in interstellar space since 2012, still phoning home. Voyager 2 (1977) visited all four giant planets — the only probe ever to do it. Both carry the Golden Record: the sounds of Earth, in case someone finds them.
